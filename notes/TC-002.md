# TC-002 — Every day at nine

**Tracked revision:** `2026-09-25.1` (25 September 2026)  
**Problem:** civil-time recurrence  
**Canonical page:** <https://tntresearch.co.uk/temporal-club/notes/002-every-day-at-nine.html>  
**Case data:** [tc-002.json](../cases/tc-002.json)

“Run at 09:00 every day in Europe/London” describes a local civil-time recurrence. “Run every 24 hours” describes a fixed elapsed interval. Across the UK's spring clock change on 29 March 2026, the first interpretation gives 09:00 GMT on 28 March (`09:00Z`) followed by 09:00 BST on 29 March (`08:00Z`): 23 elapsed hours. Adding 24 hours instead gives 10:00 BST on 29 March.

The user's intent determines which schedule is right. A system that stores only the next UTC instant cannot recover that intent. It needs the recurrence rule, local time, zone identity, and a policy for later rule changes. To reproduce an earlier computation exactly, it may also need the generated occurrences or the rule-set version used at the time.

The synthetic case states its offsets directly from the [published UK clock-change rule](https://www.gov.uk/when-do-the-clocks-change). It does not claim a pinned IANA time-zone database release. The challenge is to provide a recurrence whose intended policy cannot be recovered from the stored data.
